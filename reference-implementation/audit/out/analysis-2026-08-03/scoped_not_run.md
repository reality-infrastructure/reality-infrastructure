## 1. The Inverse Check — county-side holdings not present in your inventory system

Every audit to date runs in one direction: it grades what your inventory asserts against what the county recorded. The Inverse Check runs the other way. Using the county's own open-records datasets — and the exact spellings of your organization's name that the county actually uses, each one confirmed on the record during the audit — we sweep the assessor's taxpayer-of-record roll and the recorded-sale register for every parcel the county currently associates with your organization, then subtract the parcels your inventory system already tracks. What remains is a list your system cannot produce on its own: parcels the public record says you hold that nothing in your inventory is watching — each carrying its own tax, maintenance, and liability posture, and each cited to the specific county dataset, record, and retrieval date. Scope and cost, as established in our assessment: the discovery sweep itself is small (on the order of 20–40 targeted queries, minutes of runtime per county); the confirmation pass scales with what the sweep finds — at several hundred to a thousand county-side hits, roughly 2,000–3,000 record fetches, an estimated 2–3 hours of paced retrieval per county, using the same query pattern already proven on the current engagement. Every finding is reported as what the record shows, never as a conclusion about title.

## 2. The Conveyance Verification leg — recorded-transfer confirmation for disposed inventory

The completed audit confirmed, for the majority of disposition-marked records, a recorded transfer out of your organization within a year of the claimed date. Two gaps remain, both stated in the audit report: some recorded transfers name no counterparty in the open dataset (the grantee field is blank on roughly one in ten sale records), and some disposition claims matched no record at all in the transfer-declaration-derived dataset — which structurally omits certain exempt conveyance types common in land-bank work. The Conveyance Verification leg closes both: for each disposition-marked record still open, we pull the underlying recorded instrument by document number from the county recorder's index and report grantor and grantee as recorded, the instrument type, and the recording date — confirming, from the document of record, that title moved out to a third party, or reporting exactly what the record shows instead. Scope and cost, as established in our assessment: approximately 50–100 targeted document fetches, retrievable in a single paced session, each result cited to instrument number and retrieval date. Findings are statements of the recorded documents; nothing in the deliverable is a title opinion.

## 3. Tax-Sale Interest Confirmation — Clerk-records resolution of historical tax-sale exposure on held inventory

The audit's held-inventory analysis surfaced a specific, bounded question: 40 of the 99 county-confirmed-held parcels appear in the county's historical tax-sale records, carrying $206,101.76 in offered tax-and-penalty amounts of record. What the open datasets cannot say is what became of those interests — whether each certificate was redeemed, expired, or extinguished when your organization acquired the parcel. An unreleased tax-sale interest is precisely the class of title impediment that surfaces at closing and resolves through quiet-title work at market rates of $1,500–$5,000 per parcel; confirming release beforehand is the cheap version of that discovery. The Tax-Sale Interest Confirmation leg answers the question at its source: for each of the 40 parcels, we obtain the redemption or expiration status of the recorded tax-sale interest from the County Clerk's records — the office of record for redemptions — and report, per parcel, the interest as recorded, its resolution status, and the document trail, each cited to record and retrieval date. Scope and cost: approximately 40–80 targeted Clerk-record requests (the Clerk's redemption records are request-gated rather than open-data, so turnaround is set by the Clerk's response channel, not by our runtime); deliverable is a per-parcel status table in the audit's citation discipline. Findings are statements of the records obtained; nothing in the deliverable is a title opinion.
